# ሁሉም ለበጎ ነው — Everything Is for the Good

## Frame story (present day, Ethiopia)
A university student comes home after failing an exam they had been confident about, close to tears. Their mother notices, asks what happened, listens, and then begins to tell a story.

## The folktale the mother tells
Long ago a king and his faithful servant travelled to a ceremony outside their town, the king on horseback, the servant walking beside him. The horse stepped into a hole and stumbled; the king was thrown off and hurt his hand and leg. The servant calmly said: "Everything is for the good." The furious king asked how such pain could be good, threw the servant into a deep pit he could not climb out of, and rode on alone.
Further along, the king met a mysterious group of ritual worshippers searching for a person without any blemish to offer to their deity. They seized him and brought him to their place of worship, but when they found his wounds they judged him unfit and let him go.
The king remembered his servant's words, understood that the accident had saved his life, rode back, pulled the servant out of the pit and told him everything. The servant said again: "Everything is for the good." The king asked: "I see how my accident was good for me, but how was the pit good for you?" The servant answered: "If you had not thrown me into the pit, I would have been with you, and they would have sacrificed me instead."

## Return to the frame
The exam result has not changed, but the student's perspective has: a setback may carry a purpose we cannot see yet.

## Audience and intent
Primary: Ethiopian students and young adults facing academic failure or other setbacks. Secondary: Ethiopian families who know the tradition of parents passing wisdom to children through stories.
Emotional arc: sadness and empathy (opening) -> curiosity (the mother begins) -> tension (accident, capture) -> surprise (the wound saved the king) -> a second realisation (the pit saved the servant) -> comfort, hope and relief (ending).
Central message: do not lose hope the moment something goes wrong, because we cannot yet see its consequences. Recurring line: ሁሉም ለበጎ ነው ("Everything is for the good"), heard at least three times.

## Creative direction
- Amharic narration, natural and emotionally expressive, with accurate English subtitles. Narrated in the first person by the grown student remembering; the mother's and the characters' words are quoted.
- Cinematic, realistic visuals (not cartoon). A clear visual transition between the modern Ethiopian home (warm, contemporary) and the folktale (Ethiopian-inspired highland landscape, architecture and clothing, timeless and unspecific; not a specific historical king or event).
- Four characters with strong consistency: student, mother, king, servant. All fictionalised appearances; synthetic voice.
- The ritual worshippers are a fictional, invented group: hooded cloaks, torches, no symbols of any real religion or ethnic community.
- Injuries and the ritual are non-graphic: no blood or violence on screen, tension through faces, light, shadow and framing.
- Original instrumental score with Ethiopian influences (krar, masinko, washint, kebero), evolving from sadness to suspense to hope.
- Compositions centred so both 9:16 and 16:9 crops work.
