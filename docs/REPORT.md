# B2W0 AI-Content Generation: Report
**Kenean Biru** · Film: *ሁሉም ለበጎ ነው / Everything Is for the Good* · Repository: [ADD GITHUB URL]

## 1. What I set out to make, and for whom
An 87-second short in Amharic with English subtitles, based on a personal memory. After I failed an exam I had been sure I would pass, my mother comforted me with a traditional folktale: a king who falls from his horse, a servant who says "everything is for the good" and is thrown into a pit, and a group seeking an unblemished sacrifice who release the wounded king. The servant's last answer, that the pit saved him too, changed how I saw my failure.

**Audience:** Ethiopian students and young adults facing academic failure or other setbacks; secondarily, Ethiopian families who pass wisdom on through stories. They watch on a phone, vertical, often muted. **Intent:** an arc from sadness through curiosity, tension, surprise and a second realisation, ending in comfort: a setback may have a purpose we cannot see yet. The brief was written before any asset was generated (DESIGN.md §1).

**How it was made:** one validated `storyboard.json` drives a cached pipeline: script → narration → radio cut → reference sheet and keyframes → moving shots → score → assembly in 16:9 and 9:16. Providers used: Gemini 3.8 Flash-Lite TTS (narration), Cloudflare FLUX.1 schnell (images), Wan 2.2 TI2V-5B on Kaggle T4s (2 moving shots), ACE-Step 1.5 on Kaggle (score), FFmpeg (assembly). Actual spend: $0. A first prototype film (*ቀስ በቀስ*), built with local CPU models, was used to develop and test the studio; all of its evidence is kept.

## 2. What I learned from my audience
**Test 1, radio cut (3 people).** All three responded positively. One found the narration slightly slow. After discussing it we kept it: the slow pace suits a reflective memory and lets both twists land. Measurement backs this: the voice was not slowed (2.06 words/s, faster than the 1.2–1.5 words/s of earlier takes, EXPERIMENTS E22); the extra time is deliberate holds on emotional beats.
**Test 2, near-final cut (3 people).** Two viewers independently caught what no metric had: (1) the mother looked like a different woman in s02 and s03; (2) in s04 the king was not riding the horse, which contradicts the story. Changes (E28): s03 now reuses s02's own image as a push-in onto her face, so she is the same woman by construction; s04 was regenerated with explicit staging (correct on the first attempt). While fixing these I also replaced the generic centre crop with per-shot focus points, which fixed a 16:9 crop that cut the mother's head off at the eyes. Before/after frames: `outputs/review/compare_9x16.png`, `compare_16x9.png`.
**What this taught me:** the radio cut tested story and pacing, but only pictures revealed consistency and staging errors. Both errors came from separately generated images, which a viewer compares instantly and a per-image check does not.

## 3. The hardest problem and how I solved it
**Getting Amharic narration out of a 10-requests-a-day free tier without losing a line.** Gemini TTS speaks Amharic well and pauses at Ethiopic punctuation natively (E15), but the free tier allows 10 requests per day per model. I spent the main model's quota on a comparison experiment before I knew the limit (F09). I then built "one request per film": all lines in one take, split at the pauses (E16). On the real film the model left only 0.4–1.0 s between lines, the same as pauses inside lines; the split failed, and the per-line fallback used the remaining quota before voicing the last three lines (F15).
**Solution, with no further requests:** I split the single take offline with a dynamic-programming alignment that chooses the pauses whose segment lengths best match each line's expected length. I verified it independently: each recovered piece's rhythm correlated best with its own per-line recording in 7 of 7 lines (0.31 vs 0.06). The film uses this one continuous performance. The fixes are now in code: the aligner is the production splitter, the fallback refuses to start without enough quota, a quota ledger (matched exactly to the AI Studio dashboard) blocks experiments from spending the film's reserve, and an offline run can no longer overwrite hosted narration (F16).
The second-hardest problem was **Wan 2.2 on free T4s**: two out-of-memory failures (E20, E21) until layer-by-layer offloading made it fit in 2.25 GB of active VRAM (E21b), at about 7–9 GPU-minutes per 2-second clip.

## 4. Suggested practices, tested honestly
**A practice that beat my instinct: generate several candidates and choose with evidence.** My instinct was to keep the first decent output. For the score I generated three full-length ACE-Step tracks and measured each over the film's actual story phases (E26): one stayed loud to the end, one ended dark, and only seed 23 rose through the tension, peaked exactly at the surprise and resolved warmer. For moving shots I generated five Wan clips and checked them frame by frame: three passed, two hallucinated (a flame appearing, a colour melt) and were rejected, with the verdict recorded in the storyboard (E27). Earlier, the same practice caught two automatic metrics choosing the worst option (E07, E08b).
**A practice that did not work as stated: the reference sheet as the means of consistency.** I generated a character reference sheet first, as suggested, but with the providers available it could not condition the images: FLUX.1 schnell accepts no reference (and rejects a seed, F11), FLUX.2 klein's reference fields are undocumented and showed no measurable effect, and in the prototype SD-Turbo's img2img on the reference copied the portrait into every shot (E07). What actually gave consistency was explicit, fixed character text in every prompt (stating skin tone; FLUX otherwise lightened skin: E17, E24), plus reusing one image for two shots of the same person (E28). The sheet still served as the visual standard every keyframe was checked against.

## 5. Curiosity beyond the floor
* **Tigrinya and Afaan Oromo (E13):** the romaniser behind the open TTS maps Tigrinya's pharyngeal ዓ and glottal ኣ to the same letters, so the contrast is lost before the model sees it: a front-end failure that no voice tuning can fix. Afaan Oromo needs no romanisation.
* **Open vs hosted comparisons:** Gemini TTS vs MMS on the same lines (E15; blind A/B pairs prepared); FLUX vs local SD-Turbo (E17: ~8× faster, better prompt adherence, but no seed reproducibility); ACE-Step on a T4 vs MusicGen on CPU (E19: 27–31 s vs 183–243 s per candidate). A keyless "FLUX" endpoint turned out to be serving a different model (`sana` in EXIF) and drew the grandmother lighter-skinned and younger (E11).
* **A tool others can reuse:** one command per GPU stage (`python -m pipeline.remote wan|acestep`) packages inputs as a private Kaggle dataset, runs a private kernel, retrieves outputs and logs GPU time; quotas are enforced in code; a new film needs only a new storyboard.

## 6. What I would build next
1. A character LoRA (or a provider with documented reference conditioning) to get identity consistency from images, not only text.
2. Wan at higher resolution on a 24 GB GPU: my clips are 512 px and measured 61–85 % of the stills' sharpness (E27).
3. A blind native-speaker test of Gemini vs MMS narration with five or more listeners (pairs ready in `outputs/experiments/e15_gemini_vs_mms/blind_ab/`).
4. A simple web form where a relative tells a story in Amharic and receives a draft storyboard, using the script stage built here.
